# Advanced composition and AI

Use this reference only for requests involving elaborate composition, VFX, motion design, or generated assets. Do not make this workflow a requirement for ordinary editing.

For emphasis zooms, flashes, shakes, glitches, speed ramps, `xfade` transitions, color treatment, animated captions, or synthesized SFX that FFmpeg can handle directly, use [effects and finishing](effects-and-finishing.md). This document covers composition, tracking, 3D, and generated media.

## Decision and preparation

- Tie each effect to a purpose in the scene: demonstrate, contextualize, create atmosphere, or connect shots. Identify the affected sections, art direction, assets, available time, and expected delivery before selecting tools.
- Create a storyboard or animatic proportional to the complexity. Mark what is recorded material, illustration, or generated reconstruction. In documentary and commercial work, never present reconstructions as real footage or decorative graphics as evidence.
- Check rights, attribution, and service terms for every asset. Paid tools, external generation, uploads, and media acquisition require applicable authorization.

## Prototype and acceptance criteria

Before applying an expensive effect to the full piece, test a representative section. Define what must remain stable, such as a face, product, text, or framing. Reject drift, deformation, exposed edges, and unreadable information. Simplify the effect or replace the asset when the prototype fails. Record the shot, tool and version, and reason for accepting or rejecting it.

## Techniques

- **Mixed media and 2.5D parallax:** separate foreground, subject, and background only when the material supports it. Verify mattes, edges, areas exposed by motion, and readability.
- **Motion transitions:** coordinate direction, framing, and speed across shots. Use match cuts or speed ramps only when the footage supports them. Inspect the result frame by frame around the transition.
- **Tracking and compositing:** evaluate tracking quality, exclude bad points, and check alignment throughout the shot. Masks, lighting, shadows, and blur should match the scene. Simplify or request different footage when occlusion or low detail prevents a stable result.
- **Infographics and 3D:** use typography, palette, objects, and motion consistent with the identity. Verify every number against its source and do not use decorative graphics to make unsupported claims look proven.
- **Motion design:** use easing, anticipation, and overlap when they serve the intent. Linear movement is also valid. Scale motion and timing to the display size and reading requirements.
- **Safe areas:** check current platform guidance and a destination preview. Organic posts and ads may have different overlays. Protect faces and essential information when reframing.
- **Color:** identify input, working, and output spaces. Use scopes as diagnostic tools. Color tags describe an image but do not perform a conversion. Verify a representative sample on the destination.
- **Sound:** build dialogue, SFX, background, and music only when each layer contributes. Diagnose low-frequency noise and voice competition before filtering or ducking. Adjust by listening and measurement.

## Generated-media integration

When the brief requests generation and the environment permits it, define each shot's narrative purpose, authorized visual reference, framing, duration, movement, and format. Inspect consistency, anatomy, text, licensing, and factual suitability before editing the asset into the piece. Treat upscaling, interpolation, and generated effects as optional and reject artifacts that damage the content.

Do not expose private material to external services without applicable authorization. Deliver generated assets or an editable project only when requested or useful and available. Report the tools used, material synthetic elements, limitations, and which technical, visual, and listening checks were actually performed.
